Nearly 1 in 4 small business CEOs are burned out.

Why it matters: Burnout destroys concentration, decision-making, and ultimately your business—but small behavior changes can reverse it.

By the numbers:
- 24% of small business CEOs report frequent burnout (WSJ/Vistage Small Business Index, June 2025)
- 72% of founders say the grind damages their mental health (Startup Snapshot survey)

The reality: Tracy Klopfenstein bought an ice cream shop in Goshen, Indiana. "The first two years were the worst. We worked all the time. My mind would spiral at night. I had an absence of hope."

Clinical psychologist Chandler Chang (Therapy Lab): "Your concentration is affected. You feel depersonalized—like you've become a work machine."

What actually works:
- Check in hourly. Ask yourself if you can keep going. Learn your limits.
- Shift your mindset. Replace "I'm going to bomb" with "I wonder how this will go." You're building without a map.
- Consider medication. Klopfenstein: "A low dose antidepressant made a world of difference. This is a chemical thing."
- Break isolation. Jamie Erickson (Poppy's, Brooklyn) went from multimillion-dollar events to zero during COVID with a 9-month-old at home. Her fix: monthly check-ins with one friend, plus a WhatsApp group of female entrepreneurs.
- Make business changes. Erickson pivoted to a neighborhood cafe. Klopfenstein hired managers before expanding. Both now exercise regularly.

The bottom line: Exercise, sleep, connection, and structural changes beat pushing through. Your business needs you functional, not broken.
