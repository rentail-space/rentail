## What to prepare before making contact

Before you call your first shopping center, assemble these materials so you can respond quickly when leasing managers ask questions:

**Business overview document**: One page describing what you sell, your target customer, your brand story, and why temporary retail makes sense for your business model. Include photos of your products and any previous retail locations you've operated.

**Financial projections**: Even for a three-month lease, property managers want to see projected monthly revenue, average transaction size, and expected foot traffic conversion rates. Use conservative estimates. If you claim you'll do $100,000 in monthly sales from a 400-square-foot kiosk, nobody will believe you.

**Timeline requirements**: Specific move-in and move-out dates. "Sometime in November" doesn't work. "November 1st through January 15th" gives the leasing team concrete dates to check against their availability calendar.

**Space specifications**: Required square footage range, preferred location within the center (near anchor stores, food court adjacency, main corridor), and any special requirements like electrical capacity for refrigeration or water access for sampling.

**Budget parameters**: Total budget including base rent, percentage rent if applicable, utilities, insurance, and any tenant improvement costs. If your absolute maximum is $5,000 per month all-in, say that upfront. It saves everyone time if the center's minimum is $8,000.

**References**: Contact information for previous landlords or property managers if you've leased retail space before. First-time retailers can substitute supplier references or business credit references.

Having this ready means you can answer questions immediately during your first call instead of promising to "get back to them" and losing momentum.

## Making first contact that gets results

Once rentail.space provides your prioritized list of centers with contact information, your first call should accomplish three objectives: confirm space availability, qualify mutual fit, and schedule a property tour.

Call during business hours mid-week. Tuesday through Thursday between 10 AM and 3 PM typically works best. Avoid Mondays (leasing teams catch up from the weekend) and Fridays (people check out mentally after lunch).

When you reach the leasing office, use this framework:

"Hi, I'm [name] and I operate a [product category] business. I'm looking for [square footage] of temporary retail space for [timeframe]. I found your center through rentail.space and your property looks like a strong match for my customer demographic. Do you currently have any short-term availability during that period?"

This opening accomplishes several things efficiently. You immediately identify yourself as a qualified prospect with specific needs. You show you've done preliminary research by mentioning the platform that matched you to their center. You ask a direct question that requires a real answer.

If they have availability, the conversation shifts to logistics: exact square footage, location within the center, pricing structure, lease terms, and next steps. Request a property tour within the next week while you're on the phone. Leasing velocity matters in retail real estate. Wait two weeks to schedule a tour and the space might lease to someone else.

If they don't have current availability, ask about future openings and whether they maintain a waitlist for temporary tenants. Even a "no" call builds a relationship for future opportunities.

## What happens after initial contact

Assuming your first call goes well and you schedule a property tour, the next phase involves site evaluation, lease negotiation, and final approval through the center's tenant committee.

Property tours should focus on traffic patterns during peak hours (weekends and early evenings), sight lines from main corridors, proximity to complementary tenants, and infrastructure needs for your specific business. Take photos and notes. Visit during the time of day when your target customers typically shop.

Lease negotiations for temporary space are more streamlined than traditional retail leases but still involve multiple variables: base rent, percentage rent thresholds, common area maintenance fees, insurance requirements, signage restrictions, and operating hour mandates. Everything is negotiable within reason. If a center quotes $4,500 per month and your budget caps at $4,000, counter-offer. The worst they can say is no.

Most shopping centers require tenant committee approval for new leases, even short-term ones. This committee typically includes the property management company, ownership representatives, and sometimes anchor tenant representatives. They review your business plan, financials, and brand presentation. Approval timelines range from one week to four weeks depending on committee meeting schedules.

Once approved, you'll receive a lease agreement to review. Read every line. Pay special attention to exclusivity clauses (preventing the center from leasing to direct competitors), renewal options, and early termination conditions.
